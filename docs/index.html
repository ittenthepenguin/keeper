The problem

The footage that would settle a crash is often gone before anyone asks for it.

Dashcams loop over old clips. Vehicle data is lost once a car is moved. By the time an adjuster or attorney asks, the record is overwritten, handed over in part, or hard to open. We heard this repeatedly across our customer-discovery interviews.

11discovery interviews with carriers, SIU, defense counsel, forensic experts, adjusters and fleets
0confirmed AI-altered dashcam cases. Lost and incomplete footage came up instead.
Hoursbefore some vehicle data is gone for good, in trucking defense practice

Losing evidence after the duty to preserve it arises can bring court sanctions. Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 37(e). Quotes are from our interviews, attributed by role.

The solution

A small box beside your dashcam that keeps the original.

Keeper is designed to preserve an incident’s original footage, attach a signed acquisition record, and hand it off as ordinary files. Our current browser prototype demonstrates reporting, review, and sharing while the device workflow is being tested.

  1. 01

    Report

    Press Report incident on your phone when it's safe. It saves a record. It does not call emergency services.

  2. 02

    Keep

    The proposed device workflow preserves the original segments and signs their acquisition record before handoff.

  3. 03

    Hand off

    The intended package combines original video, a signed record, and a check. The current demo exports an unsigned example.

  • Not a fleet telematics platform
  • Not a deepfake detector
  • Not a claims system

Where we are: browser recording, review, and sharing are available in the demo. BeagleBone capture, signing, wireless delivery, and on-device proofs still require hardware validation; signing, proof, and remote backup in the demo are simulated.

Who it's for

Built for the driver. Designed for the person who has to believe the footage.

Primary user

Drivers with a dashcam

  • Drive a personal vehicle and already run a consumer dashcam
  • Outside fleet platforms such as Samsara and Motive, which upload crash clips for commercial fleets
  • Bought the camera hoping never to need it, so setup has to be invisible
Recipients

Adjusters, investigators and attorneys

  • Decide whether footage can be relied on in a claim or a lawsuit
  • Work in email, PDFs, MP4s and carrier portals, not proprietary players
  • Need the complete original, not just the outward-facing clip
Buyer we're testing

Personal-injury law firms

  • Could give Keeper to clients and pay per case
  • Send preservation demands because footage gets overwritten
  • Not validated yet: this is what our pilot is for

What makes it different

Three things a dashcam alone doesn't do.

Captured before it's overwritten

Designed to preserve an incident’s original segments before the camera records over them.

Why it mattersFootage and vehicle data disappear within hours.

A signed record of where it came from

Designed to attach a signed acquisition record: which device copied which files, and when. The current demo record is unsigned.

Why it mattersHow footage was acquired decides what can be proven later.

Opens anywhere, no account

Share a recipient link or download an ordinary ZIP with the video and an example acquisition record. Recipients do not need an account.

Why it mattersRecipients work in email and ordinary files.

The proposed signed-record check would show agreement with an acquisition record. It would not prove scene truth or fault. The demo’s signing and proof states are illustrative.
